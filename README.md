# AI in Support

Writing about what actually happens when AI enters a support organization — not the pitch, but the operational reality.

The work here covers three connected areas: how to deploy AI in support, how to operate it once it's live, and how to develop the people running it. Each series builds on the last.

---

## The Argument

AI reduces routine ticket volume. It does not reduce work. It shifts work.

When the easy tickets fade, what remains is harder. Exceptions. Appeals. Multi-system failures. High-emotion customers who already tried self-service. The blast radius of a wrong answer grows. Meanwhile, someone has to keep the system running — monitoring behavior, maintaining knowledge, tuning routing, debugging failures. That work rarely gets staffing, time, or ownership. It lands on the same team already handling customers.

Support now runs two jobs. Job one: resolve customer issues and protect trust. Job two: operate the system resolving customer issues.

The teams that lead this shift are the ones that name job two, staff it, and measure it.

## Series

### [AI in Support](./ai-in-support/)
*7 articles · November – December 2025*

The foundation. How to deploy AI in a support organization without creating the problems you were trying to solve. Covers use case selection, agent training, measurement, change management, governance, and what happens after rollout.

### [AI Ops for Support Leaders](./ai-ops-for-support-leaders/)
*10 articles · January – March 2026*

The operating layer. Once AI is live, someone has to run it. This series covers the quality loop, prompt discipline, risk and hallucinations, testing, defect taxonomy, ownership, release management, incident response, and the operating system that keeps AI stable over time.

### [Support Skills for the AI Era](./support-skills-for-the-ai-era/)
*In progress · March 2026–*

The people layer. As routine work gets automated, the remaining work gets harder. This series covers how support staff need to think and work differently — context awareness, source verification, translating between customer language and system logic, and what it means to be an operator of AI, not just a user of it.
